Steel legs make live edge tables simple to build, but where you mount them decides who can sit where. Put them too close to the ends and nobody fits at the ends. Put them too far in and you lose a seat along each side.

## The numbers that matter

- **Width per person along a side:** 24″ is the usual minimum; about 26″ (a 20″ chair plus elbow room) is comfortable.
- **Knee room at an end:** someone sitting at the end needs about 12″ between the end of the top and a leg frame that spans the width, and 14″ is comfortable.
- **Width at the ends:** an end seat needs the top to be at least 24″ wide there.

## Working it out

U, trapezoid and X legs span the full width of the table, so the side seats have to fit *between* the two leg frames:

> space along a side = table length − 2 × (leg inset + half the tube width)

With 2″ tube legs on an 84″ slab:

| Legs in from each end | Between the legs | Seats |
|---|---|---|
| 5″ | 72″ | 2 per side (36″ each), no end seats |
| 15″ | 52″ | 2 per side (26″ each) **and one at each end** |

Moving the legs in to 15″ costs nothing along the sides and adds two seats at the ends. That's the sweet spot for most 7′ tables.

Hairpin legs are different: they sit at the corners, so the end chair's knees go *between* the two hairpins and you can keep them close to the ends.
